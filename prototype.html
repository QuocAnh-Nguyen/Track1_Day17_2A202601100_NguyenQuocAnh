Gradient Descent

Gradient descent is a first-order iterative optimisation algorithm used to find a local minimum of a differentiable function. At each step, the algorithm moves in the direction of the steepest descent — the opposite of the gradient — to progressively reduce the loss.

Update rule

θt+1 = θt − η · ∇J(θt)

where θ is the parameter vector, J the cost function, η the learning rate, and ∇J(θt) the gradient of the cost with respect to the parameters at step t.

Intuition: the gradient as a direction

The gradient vector points in the direction of the greatest rate of increase of the function. Consequently, to descend toward a minimum we step in the opposite direction — the negative gradient — proportional to the learning rate.

Why it matters

  • The gradient encodes both magnitude and direction of steepest ascent in parameter space.
  • A small learning rate makes progress slow but stable; a large one can overshoot or diverge.
  • The gradient is derived analytically from the partial derivatives of the cost function.
